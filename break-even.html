“Usage limits apply”

Every leaderboard prices models through APIs and says so. Every cost tracker meters requests and cannot see a flat seat. The thing people actually buy — a subscription with a limit nobody states — sits between the two, unmeasured. This page is the arithmetic of that gap: what the providers disclose, where a subscription starts beating the metered price, and the one break-even anchor a provider has published about its own plan.

Subscription vs API

Where a subscription starts winning.

Under PAYG, task number 400 costs the same as task number 1. Under a subscription, every additional task inside the allowance drives the effective cost per task down — and every unused unit of allowance is money spent on nothing.

The whole curve rests on three numbers, and moves if you move any of them:

cost per task  = price ÷ (tasks × utilization) = $20 ÷ (200 × u)
break-even    = price ÷ (tasks × API cost per task)
              = $20 ÷ (200 × $0.35) ≈ 29%

Use a plan below 29% of its allowance and PAYG was cheaper. The catch is the middle term: for most plans, “tasks at full utilization” is exactly the number the provider does not publish — which is why the staircase above, not this curve, is the finding.

A subscription worth $2,000 of API compute is worth $0 of API compute if you use it twice a month.
